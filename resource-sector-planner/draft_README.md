# Resource Corps: Sector Planner

## High concept

A single-player science-fiction resource-management game about developing a dependable supply network for the Galactic Federation. The player begins as the Planner for a newly assigned resource sector, receives an initial allocation of people and equipment, and earns further allocations by meeting development milestones.

Planning alternates with short field assignments. The player coordinates production and deliveries across planets, then joins a local operation to investigate a problem or understand the work behind a report. Earth supplies crab: one assignment may involve captaining a crab boat, another supplying bait or surveying the grounds.

The central pleasure is learning how an operation works, putting that understanding into a plan, and seeing the plan deliver something people need.

| Product | Direction |
| --- | --- |
| Platform and price | Steam, about $9.99; mobile adaptation may follow |
| Presentation | Simple vector graphics, charts, instrument views, vessels, and readable reports |
| Play structure | Sector planning and occasional local operations |
| Sector duration | A proposed 5–10 hours for a first successful completion; content and pacing need playtesting |
| Completion | A sector that fulfills its delivery commitments within its operating and ecological limits and can be handed over |

## Society and the player's position

Basic needs are provided throughout the Federation. Adults who choose to work begin with two years of voluntary service in the Labor Core. Vocational opportunities are advertised and open to those who can be accommodated.

Labor Core intake is coordinated with the positions people leave through retirement, transitions to advisory work, and vocational changes. Short-term community-need reassignments also affect the availability of people and the need for recruitment. Population accounting and assessment of society's needs happen outside the player's role.

The player has completed two years in manufacturing, chosen the Resource Corps, and completed its training. The game begins with their first assignment: planning the development of a resource sector.

Planning is an entry position. Experienced field personnel are senior to the player and provide practical advice. Advancement takes people farther from the center and closer to the work. Temporary field assignments let the player learn from those seniors before taking on greater field responsibility.

At sector level, the player works with available teams, their agreed assignments, and requests for support. Recruitment policy, retirement decisions, and the provision of basic needs belong to the wider society. The game does not require a population-management simulation.

Planets are either uninhabited or their inhabitants participate in the Federation. Extraction operates within the arrangements and ecological limits established for each location. Earth is a member planet, and its crab fishery is one resource operation in the sector.

## The two scales of play

### Sector planning

Galactic distribution supplies a delivery brief: required quantities, a collection window, and relevant limits. For a food-sector scenario, the brief specifies carbohydrate, fat, and protein requirements. Products contribute different amounts toward those requirements; several combinations should be capable of fulfilling the brief.

The player assigns available capacity, commissions surveys, schedules supplies and transport, and keeps operations maintained. Reports show what was delivered, what remains uncertain, and where plans need attention. Milestones release additional resources or capabilities.

The operating budget represents an allocation of capacity and supplies. The player is responsible for using that allocation well. Personal wealth is not the measure of success.

### Local operations

A senior may invite the player to cover an assignment, accompany a survey, or investigate a persistent discrepancy. The player temporarily controls one operation with a bounded objective and an agreed resource allowance.

For Earth crabbing, the player reads the chart and sounder, chooses pot placements and a hauling plan, and compares catches. On returning to sector planning, those observations update the operation's forecast or support a revised method.

Established teams can carry out routine work competently. A local assignment should provide discovery, enjoyment, or a lasting improvement that remains useful after the player hands control back. The first sector needs one well-developed field activity before additional activities receive their own controls and simulation.

## Planning decisions and administrative work

The proposed sector has six recurring families of planning decisions. A family is a related kind of choice, not a single slider or a count of every adjustable value.

| Decision family | What the player chooses | What informs the choice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where to operate | Sites to work, rest, or investigate | Survey confidence, recent yields, local limits, and travel |
| What work to assign | Harvesting, surveys, supply runs, or transport | Team and vessel availability, capabilities, and commitments |
| When and where to deliver | Cargo mix, route, and departure window | Distribution needs, capacity, storage, and arrival forecasts |
| How to allocate supplies | Distribution of power, operating supplies, and reserves | Planned consumption, lead times, expiry, and uncertainty |
| When to maintain and rest | Protected windows for equipment and teams | Condition, agreed availability, and upcoming work |
| What to develop next | A survey, refit, facility improvement, or request for more capacity | The current bottleneck and evidence that the improvement will help |

The opening exposes three families: choose a site, assign a team, and arrange a delivery. Initial supplies and maintenance plans have serviceable defaults. Later situations create reasons to reconsider those defaults before introducing their controls.

At full scope, six families can remain available while only two or three unresolved problems need active attention. A stable supply route continues working. The player revisits it when its assumptions change or they choose to improve it.

Reports record routine facts automatically. A useful report places the expectation beside the result, identifies its source and age, and offers a relevant next action. For example: a crab shipment fell short; the catch report and departure record let the player distinguish reduced catch from a late vessel. The response may be a survey, a route change, or a revised commitment.

Advice calls should respond to a decision or visible difficulty. A senior can point out that the plan has no maintenance window or relies on an old survey, explain why it matters, and leave the revision to the player. Calls can be replayed, deferred, and read as text. Requests to change an assignment are negotiations with the people doing the work.

## Proposed scope for one 5–10-hour sector

These quantities are design hypotheses for a prototype. They are not established limits on what players can understand, and they are not measured averages from the reference games.

| Element | Initial target |
| --- | --- |
| Planning decisions | Six recurring families; three introduced at the opening |
| Active problems | Usually two or three that require a current judgment |
| Geography | Three planet-level suppliers, introduced gradually; Earth has a small chart of crab grounds |
| Operating assets | Four to six teams or vessels at full development, with dependable standing assignments |
| Distribution requirements | Three food requirements; start with a simple contribution toward the brief |
| Supply accounting | Three broad families: power, operating supplies, and replacement parts; reveal local detail only when it affects a decision |
| Sector milestones | Four substantial accomplishments, including completion |
| Capability additions | Four to six useful additions, including better information or delegation tools |
| Discoverable relationships | Six to eight designed opportunities to test an explanation and improve a plan |
| Field activity | One fully playable crabbing system, reused for several distinct assignments |
| Disruptions | One main late scenario that tests familiar relationships, with room for recovery |

The three nutritional requirements are delivery outcomes. The player adjusts the product mix and supply plan; individual diets and Federation population needs are assessed elsewhere. Similarly, operating supplies can remain one planning category while bait becomes a concrete resource during a crabbing assignment.

Mining can initially appear as a supplier of replacement parts or equipment inputs. A separately playable mine should earn its place through a distinct decision and a production budget. It is additional game scope even though it uses the same local-operation view.

### An example 7½-hour first completion

The times below are elapsed player time, including reading, planning, pauses, and field assignments. They describe a possible pacing budget, not timed gates. Skilled players may finish sooner; players who investigate alternatives may take longer. Advancement depends on outcomes.

| Approximate time | Main problem | Learning or capability payoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–30 minutes | Understand the brief and arrange a first small delivery | Complete a plan-to-result cycle; see how a shipment contributes to the requirement |
| 30–90 minutes | Compare crab grounds and investigate a disappointing return | Use a field assignment and catch comparisons to improve an explanation; gain a better reporting tool |
| 1½–3 hours | Coordinate a second supplier with limited transport and team availability | Discover that dependable production can still miss a delivery; establish a recurring assignment |
| 3–5 hours | Add the third supplier and fulfill a balanced delivery brief | Choose among product mixes and development proposals; account for a location's recovery needs |
| 5–6½ hours | Adapt to changed yield, a supply delay, or a maintenance interruption | Reallocate familiar resources and test the assumptions behind the plan |
| 6½–7½ hours | Demonstrate dependable supply and prepare a handover | Complete the sector with maintained equipment, agreed assignments, and adequate reserves |

Every stage should include choosing a plan, seeing a result, and applying what the result teaches. More reading or a longer wait for the same shipment should not be the way to stretch a five-hour sector to ten hours.

### Reward and discovery pacing

Use several timescales, with each serving a different purpose:

- **Immediately after an input:** show the commitment, cost, schedule, and relevant forecast. This confirms what the player has arranged; it need not reveal an uncertain outcome.
- **Within roughly 5–15 minutes during active operations:** aim for useful evidence, a visible improvement, or a result that supports the next choice. The player should have a way to advance past idle travel and waiting.
- **Across roughly 15–30 minutes:** aim for a complete investigation or planning problem: compare alternatives, commit, inspect the result, and apply the lesson. Some episodes will be longer.
- **Across larger milestones:** release the four to six capability additions unevenly, when a new tool or allocation answers an experienced need. An addition may simplify routine work rather than introduce another system.

These intervals are starting points for testing. A quiet stretch can be enjoyable; a reward notification cannot compensate for a decision the player does not care about. Knowledge is a payoff when the player can use it, even without an unlock animation.

Possible capability additions include a survey assignment, clearer comparisons, standing supply orders, a specialized vessel allocation, or a choice of refit. Avoid awarding extra responsibilities and extra upkeep every time the player succeeds.

## What the reference games show

### Method and limits

This is a documentation-based comparison of Civilization VI's base-game systems, the original Cities: Skylines, SimCity (2013), and Against the Storm. Manuals and developer explanations establish controls, dependencies, and reward triggers. They do not establish how often a typical player experiences discovery over five or ten hours. No timed playthrough study has been performed here.

These families can contain many adjustable values and repeated objects. Ten buildings do not necessarily create ten new kinds of decision, but ten buildings that each need frequent attention can still create substantial work. Scope depends on both the number of relationships to learn and how often they demand intervention.

### Useful design patterns

**Civilization VI: connect activity to future capability.** Its terrain-sensitive city development and activity-driven research boosts link present choices to what becomes possible. For the sector, a field investigation could establish evidence that supports a new operating method or equipment allocation. This is an application of the pattern, not a claim that every investigation needs a research reward.

**Cities: Skylines: reveal responsibilities through progress.** The population milestone system makes new services and areas available as a city develops. For the sector, milestones can release capacity in manageable stages. Our additional rule is to provide time to use and understand the addition before introducing the next responsibility.

**SimCity: make reports actionable.** Its manual connects category statistics and data maps to diagnosing the city, and specialization guidance to a chosen direction. For the sector, a shortfall report should lead to the observations and controls needed to investigate it. An overall red warning is insufficient on its own.

**Against the Storm: make accomplishments change the available plan.** Its documented tutorial's first wood-gathering order supplies workers, a gathering improvement, and reputation that leads to another blueprint choice. Its developer also describes alternative recipes as a way to keep constrained building choices usable. For the sector, a milestone can offer several viable development options instead of one mandatory chain. The official walkthrough documents an older tutorial version, so its exact rewards should be treated as a design example rather than a promise about the current build.

The common opportunity is to let several familiar systems create a new problem together. A second delivery source need not introduce a new set of controls if it changes how the existing vessel, schedule, and supplies must be allocated. A technology tree's size is not a useful content target by itself.

### What can be said about duration and reward counts

In a 2023 interview, Against the Storm designer Michał Ogłoziński describes planning for individual games of about two hours. He also explains that the team reduced six proposed reputation routes to three because six required more systems than they could deliver, and that a run ends before a stable settlement becomes monotonous. Those are reported design intentions, not measured completion times for all players. [Designer interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/how-against-the-storm-managed-to-mix-city-building-and-roguelite-play).

For this sector, the inference is to build several complete planning arcs within the five to ten hours. Each milestone should let the player experience an accomplishment before the next stage changes the problem. Earlier arrangements can persist and become part of the next plan.

For a separate numerical comparison, Cities: Skylines II's launch progression design specifies 20 milestones, with rewards including development points, expansion permits, and access to services and management options. Players choose how to spend development points. This is distinct from the original game's population-based progression in the table above. Neither design specifies a universal interval in player minutes between milestones. [Colossal Order's progression diary](https://colossalorder.fi/news/development-diary-10-game-progression/).

These examples establish that duration, reward opportunities, and the number of systems can be deliberately constrained. They do not establish that a five-hour level needs a particular number of unlocks. Our four milestone arcs and four to six capability additions remain a testable proposal.

## Milestones and completion

The proposed four milestones are:

1. **Deliver:** complete the first assignment with its resource use accounted for.
2. **Repeat:** establish a dependable supply arrangement and show what evidence supports the forecast.
3. **Coordinate:** fulfill the balanced brief across several suppliers while respecting operating and ecological limits.
4. **Hand over:** demonstrate that the arrangement can continue through ordinary variation, with maintained assets, agreed responsibilities, and adequate reserves.

Further allocations depend on those accomplishments. Each allocation should address a limitation the player understands and leave a choice about how to proceed. Greater capacity is useful when it supports a wanted plan; it need not raise the delivery target automatically.

Completion requirements are visible before the final stage. A player can succeed with a sound plan that leaves room for improvement. A recommendation for future field work can recognize their development without making another management level necessary to finish this sector.

The 5–10-hour estimate applies to one sector. The number of sectors in a release remains a separate scope decision. Build and time one complete sector before committing to several at this length.

## Prototype and evaluation

Start with one delivery requirement, Earth crab grounds, three operating assets, one short field assignment, and a single constrained transport route. Implement the first three planning families and a functioning report-to-revision cycle before expanding to the full sector.

A timed playtest should record:

- Each consequential decision, the information consulted, and what alternatives the player considered.
- Moments when the player changes an explanation, demonstrates a relationship, or gains a useful capability.
- Which problems need attention concurrently, how often stable arrangements are revisited, and why.
- Time spent planning, reading, operating locally, waiting, and repeating work already understood.
- Advice requests and whether the advice helps the player make their own next decision.
- How the player explains the finish and whether the final task feels earned.

Ask for explanations at a few natural breaks. Compare what players predict before a result with what they choose afterward. A notification counts as a capability reward only if it makes an understood option available; a discovery opportunity counts as learning only when the player shows or explains the relationship.

Test with newcomers to planning games and with experienced players. Compare a first attempt with a repeat attempt to separate interface learning from enduring decisions. Use the observed time and decisions to revise the scope and pacing targets. If play becomes repetitive before five hours, change the situations or shorten the sector. If players remain occupied with basic controls at ten hours, simplify the interface or reduce the number of interacting demands.
